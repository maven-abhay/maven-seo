Braze and Salesforce Integration: A Practical Guide to Automated Customer Journeys

What to sync, how to connect the two platforms, and how to turn CRM data into journeys that send the right message at the right time.

7 min read By Jatin Anand, Managing Director

Salesforce usually knows the most about your customers: who they are, what they bought, when they last visited and what they agreed to receive. Braze is built to act on that knowledge, sending the right message on the right channel at the right moment. Connecting the two is one of the most valuable things a marketing team can do, and one of the easiest to get wrong.

Why connect Braze and Salesforce

Without an integration, marketing teams export lists from the CRM, upload them to the messaging tool and hope nothing changed in between. Messages go to people who already booked, miss people who just became eligible, or ignore an opt-out recorded yesterday. A proper integration removes that gap.

The two platforms have different jobs. Salesforce is the system of record for customers, appointments, orders and consent. Braze is a customer engagement platform that builds profiles, segments audiences and runs automated journeys across email, push, in-app messages and SMS. When Salesforce data flows into Braze reliably, journeys react to what customers actually do.

Key takeaways

  • Salesforce stays the system of record; Braze is where you act on the data across channels.
  • Sync only the identifiers, attributes, events and consent that your journeys actually use.
  • Choose between direct API sync, a data warehouse with Braze Cloud Data Ingestion, or an integration platform based on volume and freshness needs.
  • Respect consent in both systems, test with real scenarios and monitor the sync after launch.

What data to sync

The most common mistake is trying to copy everything. Start from the journeys you want to run and work backwards to the data they need. In most projects that means four kinds of data:

  • Identifiers: one stable ID shared by both systems, used as the Braze external user ID, so every update lands on the right profile.
  • Profile attributes: name, preferred store or location, language, customer type and key dates such as the last visit.
  • Events: things that happen and can trigger a journey, such as an appointment booked, an order placed or a prescription expiring.
  • Consent and preferences: which channels each person has agreed to, so Braze only sends what they allowed.

Three ways to integrate Braze and Salesforce

1. Direct API sync

Changes in Salesforce are sent to the Braze REST API, typically the user track endpoint, which creates or updates profiles and records events. Salesforce features such as Change Data Capture or platform events can push changes in near real time. This suits smaller data volumes and journeys that must react quickly.

2. Data warehouse and Braze Cloud Data Ingestion

If Salesforce data already lands in a data warehouse, Braze Cloud Data Ingestion can sync tables from the warehouse into Braze on a schedule. This scales well, keeps transformation logic in one place and lets you combine CRM data with other sources such as e-commerce or app data.

3. An integration platform

Integration platforms and customer data platforms offer connectors for both systems. They can be quick to start with, but check costs at your volume, how errors are reported and how much control you have over field mapping.

Whichever option you choose, plan for field mapping, de-duplication, error handling and monitoring from the start. Integration problems rarely show up as errors; they show up as the wrong message sent to the wrong person.

Designing journeys in Braze Canvas

Braze Canvas is the visual journey builder where triggers, waits, decision splits and messages come together. A good journey has a clear trigger, a clear goal and an exit as soon as the goal is reached, so customers never receive a reminder for something they already did.

Take a healthcare example. Salesforce knows when each customer is due an eye test. Thirty days before, Braze sends a personalised email that mentions their usual store. Three days later it checks the appointment data: customers who booked leave the journey and receive a confirmation, while those who did not get a gentle push notification or SMS, but only if they opted in to that channel.

The same pattern works for appointment confirmations and reminders, contact lens reorders, welcome series for new customers and personalised offers. Each journey is small, measurable and easy to improve.

Consent, privacy and data quality

  • Keep consent in sync both ways. An opt-out recorded in either system must reach the other quickly. Map Salesforce consent fields to Braze subscription states and subscription groups.
  • Send only what you need. Avoid syncing sensitive details, such as clinical or financial information, unless a journey truly needs them and your legal team has approved it.
  • Validate data at the edge. Check formats for email addresses, phone numbers and dates before they reach Braze, and log what was rejected.
  • Agree ownership. Decide which system owns each field so updates do not overwrite each other.

Testing and going live

  1. Test with internal users who walk through every branch of each journey on every channel.
  2. Run a small live audience first, such as one region or store, and compare results with a control group.
  3. Monitor the sync with alerts for failed updates, sudden drops in volume and growing error rates.
  4. Review results regularly, such as bookings or reorders driven by each journey, and improve the messages and timing.

Common pitfalls to avoid

  • Using email address as the main identifier, which breaks when customers change it.
  • Syncing hundreds of fields nobody uses, which slows the integration and adds cost.
  • Journeys without exit rules, so customers are reminded about things they already did.
  • Ignoring time zones and quiet hours for push and SMS.
  • No owner for the integration after launch, so silent failures go unnoticed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Braze have a native Salesforce integration?

Teams usually connect the two through the Braze REST API, through a data warehouse with Braze Cloud Data Ingestion, or through an integration platform. The right option depends on data volume, how fresh the data must be and the tools you already use.

What data should we sync from Salesforce to Braze?

Start with a shared customer ID, the profile attributes your messages use, the events that trigger journeys and each customer’s consent. Add more only when a journey needs it.

How do we handle opt-outs between Salesforce and Braze?

Map consent fields to Braze subscription states and groups, and sync changes in both directions quickly so an opt-out in either system is respected everywhere.

What is Braze Canvas?

Canvas is the Braze journey builder. It combines triggers, delays, decision splits and messages across channels into one automated customer journey.

Can you help with an existing Braze setup?

Yes. We can review your current integration and journeys, fix data issues and add new automated journeys.
